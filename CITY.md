# City brief: Melbourne

## 1. Identity

- City / district: Melbourne, Victoria, Australia — the Hoddle Grid, the Yarra, Southbank, Carlton and East Melbourne.
- One-line pitch: A river bends around a formal grid, trams climb the bridges, and a golden spire marks the arts precinct.
- What makes it instantly recognisable in a 3-second overview: the wide Yarra cutting between the Hoddle Grid and Southbank, the gold Arts Centre spire and Eureka crown, Flinders Street Station's dome and clocks, the MCG's oval and light towers, and the green exhibition dome in Carlton Gardens.
- Languages / locales: English (`en`) and Vietnamese (`vi`).
- What the city is *not*: not Sydney Harbour, not a generic waterfront CBD, not a foggy peninsula. No Opera House, no Harbour Bridge, no golden-sand beachfront. The bay is a working port edge, not a resort.

## 2. Geography (compressed)

- Orientation: north is up (`-z`). East is `+x`.
- Water: the Yarra enters from the east, runs west along the south edge of the CBD, then turns south-west into Port Phillip Bay. The bay occupies the south-west corner of the tray. The river is a real channel about 1.0–1.4 units wide, with two inhabited banks.
- Land shape: a wide, mostly flat tray with a shallow bay wedge at the south-west. North bank: Docklands, the Hoddle Grid, Carlton. South bank: Southbank, the Domain, Yarra Park and the MCG.
- Green: Carlton Gardens, Fitzroy Gardens, Treasury Gardens, Flagstaff Gardens, Royal Botanic Gardens, Yarra Park.
- Tray aspect and approximate dimensions: 24.4 × 18.4 units, rounded base, viewed from the south-east.
- Why the compression reads as Melbourne: the river runs *through* the city rather than beside it; the north bank is a tight orthogonal grid, the south bank is a looser cultural/park edge; the MCG sits east on the south bank, the Exhibition Building north in Carlton, and the bay is a working western edge.

## 5. Street pattern

- Dominant pattern: the Hoddle Grid, a tight orthogonal grid rotated about 5° against the tray, cut by the Yarra; wider diagonal St Kilda Road leaves Princes Bridge; parkland boulevards in Carlton and East Melbourne.
- Major roads: Flinders, Collins, Bourke, Lonsdale, La Trobe, Victoria (east–west); Spencer, King, William, Queen, Elizabeth, Swanston, Russell, Exhibition, Spring (north–south); St Kilda Road, Royal Parade, Nicholson Street, Bridge Road, Brunton Avenue.
- Bridges: Princes Bridge (Swanston, trams), Queens Bridge (Queen Street), Spencer Street Bridge (Spencer).
- Transit: trams on Swanston, Bourke, Collins, Flinders and St Kilda Road; buses and cars on the grid; river ferries and a bay ferry; bicycles.
- Pedestrian routes: Southbank Promenade, the Yarra bank paths, Fed Square plaza, Carlton Gardens paths, the Tan around the Botanic Gardens.
- Connectivity: the grid is built per bank; the three bridges plus their approach roads connect the banks into one graph.

## 7. Transport and city life

- Signature vehicles: green-and-cream trams with pantographs; yellow taxis; grey cars; orange-and-white buses; white vans; bicycles.
- Boats: a river ferry, a punt, rowing shells on the Yarra, a bay ferry near the mouth.
- Pedestrians: commuters on the Princes Bridge, promenade walkers, Fed Square crowds, park runners.
- Street furniture, stalls, signs, trees: tram stops, green Yarra lamps, plane trees, eucalypts, benches, bins, wayfinding totems, coffee carts.
- What moves at night: trams with lit windows, car headlights, lamp pools, warm office windows, gold spire and Eureka crown lights, river reflections.
